QUESTION 4
Do the sixth condition (isolation declaration) and the diversification-request source (owner or owner’s delegate) close the “circumvention by not requesting” and “denial of isolation” gaps, or do they leave a door open? Is their wording tight?
================================================================================

These two additions are documentary controls. They record assertions. They do not close the underlying gaps in the sense of making isolation or request-integrity true. Under the treated three-grade language, they should not be described as closing those gaps evidentially. The right question is whether they close the documentary loopholes that previously let an implementer omit a request or omit an isolation statement while still looking protocol-compliant, and whether they overclaim in doing so.

Isolation declaration (RULE-019 condition 6 / §12-1 condition 6).

What it does: it makes a missing isolation statement a visible documentary defect. A review output that does not begin with a declaration that the reviewer read only the frozen package and did not explore the repository fails condition 6 on its face.

What it does not do: it does not prevent a false declaration. A reviewer (or an implementer writing in the reviewer’s name) can declare isolation and then have read other files. The text itself says this is “a recorded self-attestation, not an automated barrier.” §12-2 says isolation is not automatically guarded. §12-4 says the gateway does not automatically check context isolation. That is honest. The “denial of isolation” gap is therefore not closed as a fact-gap; it is closed as a silence-gap. Silence is no longer compliant. False speech remains possible and is labeled as self-attestation.

Wording tightness for condition 6: tight enough for a documentary protocol. It specifies where the declaration appears (the start of the output), what it must assert (read the frozen package only; did not explore the repository), and what it is not (not an automated barrier). It does not specify a required English or Arabic formula, so a vague opening sentence could be argued to satisfy it. That is a minor formula-gap, not a hole that restores proof-claims.

Diversification-request source (§12-1 closing paragraph and RULE-019 opening).

What it does: diversification under this protocol cannot be initiated by the implementer alone. The request must come from the owner or the owner’s delegate, and must be recorded (requesting party, scope, chosen provider) in the work-item row before review starts. “No diversification without a recorded request, and the implementer alone shall not decide that there is no request.”

What it does not do: it does not make diverse review mandatory for work-item closure generally. RULE-019 still opens with “when diversification of the reviewer is requested.” §12-4 still says this protocol is not an automated barrier and is not used as a commit-rejection rule. If the owner never requests diversification, diverse review simply does not occur, and other work items can still close under ordinary work-control rules. That is the on-request design, now with a recorded-request precondition when the protocol is used.

The phrase “the implementer alone shall not decide that there is no request” is the attempt to close “circumvention by not requesting.” Its wording is not fully tight:

- It forbids a unilateral implementer determination that no request exists, but it does not require a positive recorded statement of “no diversification requested” when diversification is not used. Absence of a row entry is then observationally identical to “the owner did not request” and to “the implementer never asked and nobody recorded anything.” The implementer can still proceed without diverse review whenever the owner is silent. The text does not say the owner must actively record a waiver.
- It does not define “delegate,” the form of delegation, or how a reader of the row distinguishes a valid delegate from an implementer-created proxy.
- It does not say who is forbidden from starting a review if the row is empty. In practice the implementer is the party who would dispatch the review. The sentence constrains a decision about the absence of a request, not the dispatch action itself.

So: when the protocol is invoked, a missing pre-recorded request is now a documentary non-compliance. When the protocol is not invoked, silence still means no diverse review, and that remains a lawful path under an on-request documentary protocol. The “circumvention by not requesting” gap is closed only for the case where someone wants to claim that a diverse review happened without a recorded owner request. It is not closed for the case where someone wants to avoid diverse review altogether. Given §12-4, the latter is not a violation of the protocol as designed.

Do these additions overclaim? No. Condition 6 explicitly denies that the declaration is an automated barrier. The request-source paragraph claims a recording rule, not proof that the recorded requester is in fact the owner.

Conclusion on question 4: the sixth condition closes the silence form of “denial of isolation” and leaves the false-declaration form open, with that openness disclosed. The request-source rule closes the “diverse review claimed without a recorded owner request” form of circumvention and leaves the “never request, therefore never diversify” form open, which is the on-request design. Wording is mostly tight; the “implementer alone shall not decide that there is no request” sentence is the weakest clause and does not require a recorded waiver. These remaining doors are inherent in a self-attested, on-request documentary protocol. They are not a restoration of occurrence-proof.
